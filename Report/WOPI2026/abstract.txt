Contactless vital-sign monitoring could extend screening to homes and low-resource settings, yet camera-based systems typically depend on high-grade sensors and cloud computing. We present SonicVitals, a low-cost optical–acoustic edge platform that estimates heart rate, respiratory rate and heart-rate variability using remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) and a MEMS microphone. An ESP32-S3 node integrates a GC2145 CMOS camera, an INMP441 microphone and an OLED display, timestamps both modalities on one microsecond clock and streams them over Wi-Fi with a median added video delay of 20 ms. To preserve the sub-percent blood-volume reflectance signal, the node sums raw RGB565 skin pixels in forehead, cheek and nose regions before JPEG compression, while a closed-loop controller sets sensor exposure from skin brightness rather than the whole scene, reducing saturated skin pixels from 82% to 0% under strong illumination. The host pipeline combines a five-test face-validity gate, motion-free segment selection, derivative-domain artefact suppression, CHROM pulse extraction with POS/green-channel consensus and a signal-to-noise acceptance gate; acoustic heart rate additionally requires consistent S1 beat intervals, which eliminated false detections in ambient sound (16% to 0%). On ten UBFC-rPPG subjects (30-s windows), quality-gated SonicVitals achieved a mean absolute error of 1.56 BPM (RMSE 2.73 BPM, r = 0.99, 81% coverage), versus 2.40 BPM for CHROM, 2.08 BPM for POS and 1.68–1.90 BPM for cross-dataset TS-CAN and PhysNet networks, while running on a CPU in 72 ms per window, 2–4× faster than these networks on a GPU. SonicVitals shows that trustworthy contactless sensing is achievable with commodity optics through sensor-aware computation.
